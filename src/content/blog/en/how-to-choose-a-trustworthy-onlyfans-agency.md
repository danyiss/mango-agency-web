An OnlyFans agency is legit when you can verify three things **before** signing anything: **who is behind it** (a registered company, with a legal name and jurisdiction), **what it takes** (a specific percentage, in writing) and **how you leave** (no lock-in, no handing over your accounts). If they cannot show you one of the three, the conversation ends there.

It sounds obvious, but almost every bad experience in this industry starts the same way: a direct message promising an opportunity, a very convincing call, and no document in sight. This guide is a checklist you can use as-is, with the real red flags and the exact questions worth asking.

## The quick checklist: 10 points

If you only read one section, read this one. A serious agency passes all ten:

1. It has a **registered company**, with a legal name and country you can verify.
2. It tells you **its percentage** upfront, before the sales call.
3. It charges **nothing in advance** and no fixed monthly retainer.
4. It shows you the **full contract** before you sign.
5. It states **in writing** that the account and the content are yours.
6. It never asks you to **transfer ownership** of your socials or your OnlyFans.
7. It explains **how to leave** and with how much notice.
8. It does **not guarantee** specific income figures.
9. It did **not cold-message you** promising miracle results.
10. It has a **public trail**: its own site, named people, verifiable reviews.

## What percentage does an OnlyFans agency take?

Standard 2026 ranges, by service level:

| Service | Typical share |
|---|---|
| Chatting only | 20% – 40% |
| Full management | 50% – 60% |

Two details nobody explains:

**What the percentage is calculated on.** OnlyFans already keeps 20% of everything you bill. The agency's share should be calculated on what **you actually receive** (net), not on gross. The difference is large, and you have to ask explicitly.

**What that number includes.** A 30% share covering chatting alone can cost you more than a 50% share that includes 24/7 chatting, marketing, content strategy and content protection. Compare services, not isolated percentages.

The red flag here is not a high percentage. It is **not being told the number until the call**. An agency that hides its rate until it has you on the phone is optimising to convince you, not to help you decide.

## The 6 red flags

**1. They messaged you first.** The most important one, and the most common. An agency mining Instagram DMs is chasing volume, not fit. Agencies with real results receive applications; they do not need to hunt.

**2. They ask for money upfront.** Joining fee, "setup cost", fixed monthly retainer before you earn anything. If the agency truly makes money when you make money, it does not need to charge you first.

**3. They guarantee numbers.** "We'll get you to $20k a month", "we'll get you 100k followers". Nobody can guarantee that — it depends on your content, your consistency and your audience. A reasoned projection is fine; a guarantee is not.

**4. There is no contract, or you only see part of it.** If they want you to start on a handshake, or only share a summary, stop. The full contract gets read before signing, with time to think.

**5. They ask for your password or for your accounts.** Some contracts include clauses that let the agency keep your profiles if you leave. It is the hardest damage to undo: you can lose years of audience you built.

**6. They only exist on WhatsApp.** No website, no registered company, no tax details, nobody putting their name to it. If you cannot verify who they are, you cannot hold them to anything.

## What must be in writing, without exception

- **The exact percentage**, and the base it is calculated on (gross or net).
- **What the service covers**: chatting and during which hours, marketing, content, content protection.
- **Ownership of the account and content**: yours, always, including after you leave.
- **Term and exit**: how long it runs, the notice period, and any cancellation penalty.
- **Exclusivity**: whether it stops you working with others, and in which territories or platforms.
- **Payment method and frequency**: when you get paid and how your share is evidenced.
- **Confidentiality**: what happens with your identity and personal data.

## 7 questions to ask before you sign

Copy them and send them as they are. How they answer tells you more than their entire website:

1. What percentage do you take, and is it calculated on gross or net?
2. Is there any upfront, monthly or setup cost?
3. Will you send me the full contract before I sign?
4. Who owns the OnlyFans account and the content if we stop working together?
5. How long is the commitment, and how do I exit if it is not working?
6. Which company is behind the agency? Legal name and country?
7. Who will be chatting on my account, and during which hours?

A serious agency answers all seven without flinching. If any answer turns vague, that is your answer.
