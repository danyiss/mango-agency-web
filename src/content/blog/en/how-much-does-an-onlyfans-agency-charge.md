What an OnlyFans creator usually needs is not a bit of help here and there: it is **full management**. Someone running the chatting, the marketing, the content strategy and everything else behind the account, so she can focus on what she is actually good at: creating great content. In the industry, that is charged at **50% to 70%** of what the creator receives. **We charge 50%, always, and on net.**

Then there are creators who already have traffic and a strategy and only want someone to run the chatting: there, the industry range is **20% to 40%**; we charge between 20% and 30% depending on what she needs.

We are going to lay all of it out, being as transparent as we possibly can, because it is the question we get asked most and the one fewest agencies answer. If you have searched for this before, you know the pattern: entire pages about "how much an agency charges" that end in *"it depends, contact us"*. This article does not end that way.

## What the industry charges in 2026

| Service | Typical share | MANGO |
|---|---|---|
| Full management | 50% – 70% | **50%** |
| Chatting only | 20% – 40% | **20% – 30%** |

**Full management** is the norm and what we recommend: the agency runs everything behind the account. Chatting, social media marketing, content strategy, monetization systems and leak protection.

**Chatting only** is the exception: the agency replies to your fans and sells PPV, and nothing else. Traffic, content and strategy remain yours. It makes sense for creators who already have everything else covered. In our case we charge between 20% and 30% depending on what the creator asks for: 24-hour coverage, an additional specific language, and so on. We never reach 40%.

That is why comparing bare percentages is pointless. A 30% chatting-only deal and a 50% full-management deal are not two prices for the same product: they are two different products.

## Gross or net: the detail that changes everything

OnlyFans keeps **20% of everything you bill**. If your page generates $6,000 in a month, you receive $4,800. That 20% is gone before anyone else touches the money.

The key question is: **does the agency calculate its share on the $6,000 or on the $4,800?**

- On **net** ($4,800): the agency takes its share of what you actually receive. That is the fair way.
- On **gross** ($6,000): the agency also charges on the $1,200 OnlyFans kept. A "50%" on gross is really $3,000 out of your real $4,800: 62.5% of what is yours.

Plenty of creators sign a percentage thinking net and discover on the first payout that it was gross. It is the difference between a fair deal and an unfair one, hidden in a single word of the contract. **We always calculate on net.** Ask any agency before you sign.

## What real full management includes

To know whether a percentage is expensive or cheap, look at what it covers. Full management should include, at minimum:

- **Chatting** by chatters dedicated to your account, specific hour coverage (24 hours, or just your time zone?), a messaging and PPV sales strategy, and someone supervising quality.
- **Marketing and traffic generation** on social media.
- **Personalised content strategy.** This is the most neglected part: one generic script does not fit every creator. A good team studies which niches fit you, what kind of content you enjoy filming and what you are comfortable with, and builds the strategy from there. A creator who films what she enjoys performs better and lasts longer than one following a template.
- **Fan retention** and monetization systems.
- **Protection of your content** against leaks (DMCA).

If you are charged a full-management percentage and only get chatting, you are overpaying. And if the content strategy is the same for every creator in the agency, it is not personalised, whatever the website says.

## Why almost no agency publishes its percentage

Because hiding the number until the call works commercially: first they convince you, then they tell you the price. It is a legitimate tactic, but it forces you to decide with incomplete information at the worst possible moment, with someone persuading you on the phone.

We prefer the opposite, and the reason is simple: **we do not want to waste anyone's time**, yours or ours. We want to be as transparent as we possibly can from the first minute. You know the number before talking to anyone, and you arrive at the call, if you do, with the price already accepted. We lose the creators looking for a lower percentage, and we are fine with that: they were not a fit for us, and the best outcome for both sides is finding that out as early as possible.

## The math that matters: alone or with an agency

There is an objection we hear a lot, and it deserves an answer with numbers: *"why would I give up half when I can do it myself?"*

Because the two figures are not comparable. A creator managing everything alone usually plateaus around **$600-800 a month**: not for lack of talent, but because nobody can film, edit, post, promote, answer fans around the clock and sell PPV all at once. It is a structural ceiling.

With a professional agency behind the account, that figure usually multiplies. It is not a guarantee, it depends on your content and your consistency, but it is the pattern we see again and again.

So the real comparison is not "all of $700" versus "half of $700". It is:

- **Alone:** you do all the work and keep $700.
- **With an agency:** you do far less than half the work and keep 50% of $3,000, which is **$1,500**. And that is only the starting point: a creator who comes to us with a $700-a-month account usually starts around those numbers, and once the strategy is running the whole idea is to keep climbing month after month.

More money, far fewer hours, and an account that grows instead of stalling. That is the math to run.
